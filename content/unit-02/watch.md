# Watch out

Places where your sources differ or are easy to misread. Each starts with the deck it belongs to.

- (deck 1) Lifetime value has two names in your materials: CLV (customer lifetime value) on the slides and worksheet, LTV in the Jamba Juice answers. Same idea.
- (deck 1) The 2.1 worksheet's CAC problem has no answer key. It lists $8,000 online ads, $2,000 social media, $1,500 training new employees, 400 new customers and 1,200 products sold. Reading it as ($8,000 + $2,000) ÷ 400 = $25 (training isn't an acquisition cost; products sold is a distractor). VERIFY with your teacher.
- (deck 2) Your 2.2 slides couldn't be opened, so this deck comes from your own filled-in 2.2 notes. VERIFY anything your teacher added in class.
- (deck 3) Your worksheet adds observation and A/B tests to primary research and adds census data, market reports and news articles to secondary research. The slides list fewer. Both lists are right.
- (deck 4) Product-market fit has two definitions: your Unit 1 study guide says at least 40% of surveyed users would be "very disappointed" without the product; your 2.4 slides say there is enough demand to make the business viable. Same idea, different test.
- (deck 4) The worksheet's blank "A prototype tests the PRODUCT; an MVP tests the ___" is most likely "market" (market demand). VERIFY.
- (deck 4) In Unit 1 (1.4), the MVP comes after validation and solution development. In 2.4, the MVP is used during the validation stage. Same tool, placed differently by the two lessons.
- (deck 5) Markup and margin use the same profit but divide by different numbers: markup by cost, margin by price. The same item always has a higher markup than margin.
- (deck 5) Your slides call a loss leader "a form of penetration pricing."
- (deck 6) The worksheet's blank "Using multiple channels = ___ distribution" is most likely "multichannel." VERIFY.
- (deck 6) Your class drill classifies by the final customer, so cereal sold through a grocery store to shoppers is B2C, even though the maker sells to the store.
- (deck 6) Costco: your slides call it direct and B2C (its own warehouses and website, members), even though businesses shop there too.
