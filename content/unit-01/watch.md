# Watch out

Places where your sources differ or are easy to misread. Each starts with the deck it belongs to.

- (deck 1) Product-market fit has two definitions in your materials. Your study guide: at least 40% of surveyed users would be "very disappointed" if the product disappeared. Your Unit 2 slides and notes: there is enough customer demand to make the business viable. Know both; they describe the same idea.
- (deck 1) Value capture is described two ways: "charging above the cost of production" (study guide) and "earning revenue and profit from the value created" (slides). On a number question, value captured per unit = price − cost.
- (deck 2) The candy simulation chart in your scan is hard to read next to "Oligopoly." Your 1.2 slides and quiz agree: oligopolies have HIGH barriers to entry; monopolistic competition has LOW barriers.
- (deck 3) Your 1.3 slides label regulators checking Tesla's self-driving software as a political factor, but by the slides' own definitions (political = policy priorities, legal = specific laws and regulations) it reads as legal. If a test question gives both choices, look for clues: a new law or rule a firm must follow is legal; a government's priorities or policy choices are political.
- (deck 3) A company's own new technology or hire is not a PESTEL factor (your slides' Tesla battery example and practice test question 14).
- (deck 4) The 1.4 slides could not be opened, so this deck comes only from your one-page study guide. VERIFY anything your teacher added in class.
- (deck 5) Your study guide lists only for-profit and nonprofit organizations; the 1.5 slides add social enterprises (profit plus a social or environmental goal). Know all three.
- (deck 6) There are no 1.6 Ethics slides in your Drive; this deck comes from the study guide and the ethics questions on your practice test. VERIFY anything your teacher added.
- (deck 7) Your slides teach four structures (sole proprietorship, partnership, LLC, corporation). Your study guide also splits corporations into C-corporations (double taxation) and S-corporations (pass-through, at most 100 shareholders).
- (deck 8) Your study guide names the chain sourcing → manufacturing → warehousing → logistics → consumption; your slides name the businesses (raw materials → suppliers → manufacturers → distributors/wholesalers → retailers). Same chain, different labels.
